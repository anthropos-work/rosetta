**This reading answers one question the milestone cannot proceed without: DOES THE BURN-DOWN LEG WORK?**

iter-102 paid **both** outstanding unions — 52 anchors → **98 sites, 94 repaired** — against a residual
that iter-101's band #3 estimated at **~103, itself a floor**. That is roughly half the estimated pool,
paid in one pass, with machine-graded reach of **80.4 % / 80.6 %** against the full booked sets and
effectively **100 %** against the upheld sets.

So: **the repair leg has now been exercised at scale against a residual we finally have an estimate for.**
Either a reading over the repaired tree comes back materially smaller — in which case the loop drains and
an ETA becomes derivable — or it does not, **and that is a far more important finding than the number**,
because it would say repair is not reaching the residual and no amount of running the loop faster will
close clause 5.

**Say it the right way, in every artifact.** The series 16.7 → 29.4 → 45.2 → ~103 is **four successive
corrections to an underestimate, not four measurements of a growing pool.** The pool was probably always
~100. The estimator was wrong; the replicate fixed it.

## What each band is actually risking

**#1 / #2 — the magnitude guesses, and they are NOT centred on "it will fall to zero."** Every magnitude
band on this milestone has failed, in both directions (iter-97 #1/#2 high, iter-99 #2 high, iter-101 #1
low). The honest centre for `N` is **~15**: half the estimated pool was paid, so half of iter-101's 24
would be ~12, and iter-102 added 368 lines of *unread new prose* plus its own induced defects, which push
back up. `< 8` would mean the drain is far faster than the ~103 estimate allows and the estimate is wrong
again. `> 22` is the DOES-NOT-REACH verdict.

**#3 — overlap with iter-101's 24. Read what this band is, because it is NOT what iter-101's #3 was.**
iter-101's band #3 measured overlap against an **unrepaired** pool and was therefore a clean test of
detection independence. **iter-102 repaired all 24.** So overlap now measures *repair efficacy* first and
independence only as a contaminated second: a re-found iter-101 predicate means the repair did not close
it, or closed it at one site and left a twin. `repair_reach_guard` says the upheld set was ~100 % reached,
so the honest centre is **1–2**, and **≥ 5 fails** — which would directly contradict the machine reach
grade and would be the most valuable failure on the sheet.

**#3b — this is the clean second point on the independence question.** iter-101's within-reading `m` was
**4** on a 24 union (n₁ = 20, n₂ = 8); the iter-99 × iter-101 cross-reading `m` was **6** on 28 × 24. One
measurement of independence is not a series. This band puts a second point on it from *within* one
reading, where the subject is provably identical for both passes — the one place independence can be
measured without any closed-population assumption at all. A high `m` (> 7) against a small union would say
detection is strongly correlated and every Chapman estimate on this milestone is an over-estimate; a
`m ≤ 1` would say the passes barely overlap and the residual is larger still.

**#4 — the one that says the precision drop is structural.** Four readings held 92.1 / 93.0 / 92.7 /
93.1 %; iter-99 broke it at 78.3 %, iter-101 held the break at 77.8 % raw / 80.0 % separated — **two
readings, different adjudicators, same answer**. The band is set below the historical one **on purpose**:
it predicts the break persists. **A return to 92–93 % is now the SURPRISING outcome, not the expected
one**, and it would say something about the *repaired* corpus rather than about the instrument. Reported
**twice** — raw, and with the `wrong-tree` briefing-defect class separated — because `N` is
post-adjudication and immune to the briefing defect while the upheld rate is not.

**#5 — the asymmetry, predicted to persist.** iter-101's two passes recalled **83.3 %** and **33.3 %** of
their own union: wildly asymmetric, and it failed a band that assumed they would be alike. This band
predicts asymmetry is a property of this instrument, not an accident. It FAILS if the two passes come
within 15 points of each other — which would be good news about measurement stability and would make every
per-pass recall figure in the series re-interpretable.

**#6 — the briefing defect, measured for the third time.** n=2 so far: **4**, then **1**. This reading
changes exactly one thing — the addendum now names *which* rext tree settles a claim (§5 rule 45), while
line 37 of the frozen instrument still says the wrong one. So this band tests whether an addendum can
repair a defect in a frozen instrument **without editing it**. `0` would say yes. `> 4` would say the
addendum made it worse by presenting two answers.

**#7 — set where it can fail, and the exposure grew.** iter-101 predicted ≤ 4 and got **exactly 4**.
iter-102 added **+214 anchors** to the tree (864 → 1078 by `anchor_construct_guard`'s count), so there are
materially more chances to land on the wrong construct — while the fence's reach ratio stayed flat at
~60 %. ≤ 5 holds only if the repair's new citations are as good as the old ones. A repeat at 4–5 is a pass
at the edge; ≥ 6 says wide repair degrades citation quality, which would be a first-class finding about
the repair leg itself.

**#8 — kept unchanged from iter-97/99/101, where it held three times.** The platform-drift class M257x was
created to fix is the one class with a complete fence. It has gone 7/13 → 1/20 → ~1/28 → 1–2/24. A rise
here would mean the two platform guards have a hole — and note that **five clones advanced** since
iter-101's sheet, so this reading is the first to grade the corpus against `app` at `ad9f3c49` rather than
`b948604f`.

**#9 — tests the partition, not the corpus.** The partition was **recomputed** this reading (the corpus
grew 3.6 %), and it is balanced to a 57-line spread over 1495–1552. A booked spread over 8 would say
seat-level variance dominates, which would undercut every per-reading number above it. iter-101 got 4.

**#10 — the induction rate, measured again because it is the most stable number in this milestone.**
The ~2-defects-per-repair-cycle rate has held for **five consecutive cycles**, most recently *inside the
iter documenting it* (iter-102 shipped a canonical wording sheet carrying two defects into every seat that
