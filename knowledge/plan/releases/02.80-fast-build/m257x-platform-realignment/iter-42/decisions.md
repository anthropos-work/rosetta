# iter-42 — decisions

The strategy itself is `TOK-02` in the milestone-root [`decisions.md`](../decisions.md). These are the
intra-iter decisions that shaped it.

## D-M257x-42-1 — the 18 blockers were classified by INSTRUMENT, and that is what made a revision possible

Every prior discussion of clause 5 — including iter-41's own escalation — treated the residual as a single
quantity: *18 wrong sentences, repaired by hand, at a ~50% induction rate.* Under that framing the only
available moves are *repair again* (arithmetic says it does not converge) or *change the gate* (the user has
forbidden it), which is exactly why the milestone read as stuck.

Classifying the same 18 by **the cheapest instrument that could have caught each** — read row-by-row out of
iter-41's `blocker-ledger.md`, taking the class from the ledger's own *"what is true"* column — splits them
**13 / 3 / 2**, and the 13 are the corpus contradicting **itself**. A third move appears immediately, and it
is the move the milestone has used successfully four times on other surfaces.

**The classification is a re-reading of existing evidence, not a new measurement**, and it is recorded that
way. What it did require was refusing to accept the aggregate as the unit of analysis. **An undifferentiated
count invites an undifferentiated remedy** — and the remedy the count implied (repair harder) is the one the
count itself proves does not work.

Four rows were spot-verified live before the classification was trusted (#18, #15, #9, #8) — all four still
present, all four still contradicted by the twin the ledger names.

## D-M257x-42-2 — the induced half is ONE mechanical class, and iter-41's conclusion is incomplete rather than wrong

iter-41 concluded: *"each repair injects new defects at a rate comparable to what it removes… the fixed point
of this process is not zero."* The arithmetic is correct and was the right thing to escalate on.

What the escalation did not carry is **what the injected defects ARE**. Of the 9 induced blockers, **8 are
corpus self-contradiction** — a claim repaired at one site and left standing at another. The ledger says so
in its own words, four separate times: *"added the twin row, left this one"* · *"fixed one twin row, not the
other"* · *"the contradiction is inside its own text"* · *"a blockquote spliced into a bullet list"*.

That changes what the fixed point depends on. A ~50% induction rate is a property of *the repair method*,
not a law about corpora — and this particular method fails in a way that needs **no platform knowledge to
detect**. The single induced blocker that is not self-contradiction (#13) is a cross-ref anchor, which is
the *other* fenceable class.

**Recorded because it is the load-bearing step**: had the induced half been heterogeneous — nine different
kinds of wrong — mechanization would not be available and the escalation's pessimism would stand.

## D-M257x-42-3 — the fence is built BEFORE the repair, because today's corpus is a perishable test fixture

The obvious sequencing is *repair the 18 now, fence afterwards so it does not recur.* Rejected.

A fence is only trustworthy if it has been **watched going RED** (TOK-01 rule 3; `platform-alignment.md` §8,
and the milestone's own §8 rule 5 — a mutation that does not compile is not a RED fence). Today the corpus
holds **18 blockers with a known, anchored, independently re-verified answer key** — the only such corpus
this milestone will ever have. Repair first and the fence can only be tested against a corpus nobody has
measured, i.e. it can only ever demonstrate GREEN, which is the shape §5 rule 8 exists to warn about (*a
check that SKIPS reads exactly like a check that PASSES*).

So iter-43 builds and reddens the fence and **repairs nothing**. This mirrors iter-41's own pre-commitment in
the opposite direction — it refused to repair so the *reading* would stay interpretable; iter-43 refuses to
repair so the *fence* can be falsified.

## D-M257x-42-4 — the audit instrument is NOT touched, and the reason is a measurement, not deference

The revision is a strategy change that leaves the expensive part expensive: 7 auditors, 40 files, ~9,163
lines, read top-to-bottom, per pass. The temptation to scope it is strong and has been measured twice:
iter-21's term-scoped audit converged on `11 → 5 → 2` and a full read then found **53**; iter-38 measured
that scoping to the highest-density files would have found **11 of 17** and declared the rest clean,
including the two most consequential.

**A cheaper instrument does not produce a smaller number — it produces an uncomparable one.** The whole
value of iter-41's `18` is that it is the one number in the series produced by an instrument held fixed. The
revision spends its effort on **reducing what the instrument has to find**, never on making it look at less.

## D-M257x-42-5 — the user's ruling on clause 5 is treated as settled, and no part of this revision routes around it

iter-41 escalated whether a hand-maintained corpus can satisfy a zero-blocker clause at all. The answer was:
**continue against the clause as specified.**

This tok therefore contains no proposal to re-cut, narrow, re-read, or partially satisfy clause 5, and no
step whose success condition is anything other than *a full pass returns zero*. `CHECK-M257x-iter33-derived-fact-fence`
is used because it is judged the fastest path to that reading — **not** as a substitute for it. The fence
does not grade clause 5; step 5's reading does.

Worth stating plainly, because it is the honest reframing the ruling makes available: the escalation's
premise was *"hand-maintained"*. The revision's answer is that **the corpus does not have to stay
hand-maintained** — which is a way to satisfy the clause as written, not a way around it.
