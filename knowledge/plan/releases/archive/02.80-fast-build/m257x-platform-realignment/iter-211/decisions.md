## `D-M257x-211-1` — the retraction is written into the CODE, not only into the record

`SURVEY-M257x-iter210-five-fences-scan-only-the-two-root-docs` was false. Retracting it in this iter's
`progress.md` alone would leave the next reader of `repair_leak_guard.py` free to re-derive it from the
same one-constant reading that produced it — the route was not careless, it was a plausible inference
from a real line of code.

So the retraction lives in `TheFAMILYSpellsOneScopeFourWays`'s docstring, beside the table of what the
four spellings actually resolve to, in the module that runs on every suite. `§5` iter-98: *write the
retraction in the vocabulary the fence enumerates.*

**And the retraction inherits the weakness of the claim it retracts** (`§5`): this reading is three
lines of context deep where iter-210's was one. It is recorded with the resolved sets — 114 / 114 /
114 / 92 — so the next reader grades a measurement rather than a sentence.

Two repairs were available for the A-vs-C depth mismatch:

1. **Deepen `SKILL_SOURCE_GLOB` to `**`.** Chosen. Zero cost on the real tree (A − C = 0, findings
   unchanged in both fences), and it makes the agreement structural instead of a fact about no skill
   having a nested `.md` today.
2. **Route all five A/B fences through `fence_provenance.corpus_sources`.** Not done. It is a

   `.claude` **entirely**, not just `skills/` — and iter-210's own stop condition was that a *findings*
   change is a behaviour change needing its own iter. Routed as
   `SURVEY-M257x-iter211-A-and-B-still-spell-their-own-scope`.

What the iter does buy immediately is the property iter-210 argued for: the spellings are **compared on
every run**. Sharing is better than comparing; comparing is a great deal better than a comment saying
they are kept identical.
